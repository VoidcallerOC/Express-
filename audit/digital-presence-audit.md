# Digital Presence Audit

## Owned presence

No clearly identifiable official standalone Express Pizza & Scoops website was verified. The visible direct-order destination is Slice-powered and carries Slice branding, app links, and an `Empowered by Slice` footer. It is a useful transactional property, but it is not an owned marketing home in the Forge sense. The legacy Best Pizza domain is a separate public property with a different business name and an unclear current relationship to Express. [1] [2]

## Discovery

Discovery is reasonably strong for customers already searching the brand or address because Express appears on Slice, Uber Eats, Grubhub, DoorDash, Postmates, Yelp, Facebook, and menu aggregators. Search quality is weakened by the Best Pizza/Express naming transition, conflicting phone numbers, conflicting hours, and the lack of a canonical owned domain. The NAP core is mostly consistent at 82 Pane Road, but the phone and brand fields are not fully consistent.

## Customer journey

The current path is typically **search → marketplace or Slice listing → menu → platform checkout**. It supports ordering, but it gives the business limited control over first impression, storytelling, cross-platform consistency, calls, and directions. A stronger Forge path is **search → Express-owned site → clear menu orientation → preferred order destination → call or directions**. Platforms can remain the checkout layer.

## Mobile experience

The marketplace pages make ordering possible on mobile, but the customer must interpret long menus and platform-specific labels. A branded mobile site should surface hours, phone, directions, delivery/pickup, order online, popular categories, and the pizza-plus-scoops identity above the fold. A sticky call/order bar and tap targets should be treated as core functionality.

## Content

The current transactional content is deep but repetitive and inconsistent across platforms. It lacks a stable editorial hierarchy for the business itself. The future site should summarize the offering, expose verified categories, distinguish ordering from information, and avoid duplicating a checkout menu that the POS or marketplace owns.

## Third-party dependency

Dependency is high for discovery-to-order and very high for reviews and menu pricing. The goal is not to replace these providers. The goal is to make them destinations in an owned journey instead of the first and only meaningful brand experience.

## Reputation

Available reviews are generally positive around taste, portions, value, service, and timely takeout. The evidence set is fragmented and platform-specific. The site should address legitimate friction by publishing accurate hours, an understandable menu orientation, clear contact details, and reliable directions. It should link to reviews rather than manufacture or curate fake proof.

## Audit conclusion

Express has an active digital presence but not a verified owned digital home. The gap is not “no online presence.” The gap is a fragmented, platform-led experience during a brand transition. That is a credible and respectful Forge-CT opportunity.
