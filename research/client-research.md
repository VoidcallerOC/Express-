# Client Research: Express Pizza & Scoops

## Business Identity

Express Pizza & Scoops is listed at 82 Pane Road, Newington, Connecticut 06111. Public ordering pages identify it as a pizza, American, and Italian restaurant. A Newington community update reported that “Best Pizza” changed to Express Pizza & Scoops in 2026, while older listings and the legacy website still use Best Pizza. This is a meaningful brand-transition issue, not proof of a legal name change. [1] [2] [3]

## Contact Information

The strongest repeated phone number is **(860) 665-8128**, shown on the Slice-powered ordering site, Slice menu page, Grubhub, and Uber Eats. Yelp search results for the legacy Best Pizza listing show a conflicting number, and a Facebook search result shows another number. These should not be normalized in a future site until the owner confirms the current phone. [1] [2] [4] [5]

## Hours

Published hours conflict. The Slice-powered site shows 10:45 AM–10:00 PM; Grubhub shows pickup and delivery 11:00 AM–10:00 PM; Uber Eats shows 11:00 AM–9:45 PM. A legacy Best Pizza site lists different late-night hours by day, including Friday to midnight. The future site must use a client-confirmed weekly schedule and identify holiday exceptions. [1] [2] [3] [6]

## Ordering

Express is orderable through a Slice-powered branded ordering page at `orderbestpizza.com`, plus Slice, Uber Eats, Grubhub, DoorDash, and Postmates listings. The Slice-powered page supports delivery and pickup, reports delivery of 30–45 minutes and pickup of 15–30 minutes, and links to phone and directions. The business therefore has a functioning transactional layer, but it does not yet have a clearly owned branded digital front door. [1] [2] [7] [8]

## Menu

The verified public menu spans family deals, pickup specials, pizza, specialty pizza, appetizers, salads, breads, buffalo wings, chicken tenders, calzones, stromboli, deluxe sandwiches, clubs, wraps, pita pockets, grinders, parmigiana grinders, hot dogs, paninis, quesadillas, chef specials, dinners, specialty dinners, fried dinners, kids, desserts, beverages, lunch specials, and catering. Uber Eats additionally labels soup, burgers, ice cream, and Express specialties. The full menu is large and requires information architecture rather than a single undifferentiated list. [1] [2] [9]

Representative Slice prices include cheese pizza at $12.99, gluten-free cheese pizza at $13.50, specialty pizzas generally at $15.99–$17.99, family deals at $22.99–$46.95, and cheese calzone at $12.99. Grubhub and Uber show different platform prices, so prices should not be copied into production without a current menu export or owner approval. [1] [2] [9]

## Branding

The current ordering page visibly labels the business Express Pizza and Scoops but references a logo filename `Best_Pizza.png` and links to a Facebook page using `bestpizzanewington01`. The legacy website is branded Best Pizza Newington and uses a Best Pizza logo. This creates a customer-facing naming transition that should be resolved with an approved master brand system. [1] [3]

## Photography

The legacy Best Pizza site exposes food, gallery, logo, banner, and testimonial image URLs. These are public references, but ownership, currentness, and reuse rights are unknown. The Slice ordering page also exposes product thumbnails and a logo asset. Public availability is not equivalent to client approval or license clearance. [1] [3]

## Social Presence

The Slice page links to Facebook, and search results identify `bestpizzanewington01`. The Newington Scoop community page reported the move from Best Pizza to Express Pizza and Scoops. No clearly verified Express-branded Instagram account was found in this review. Social handles, account ownership, and the preferred public naming should be confirmed. [1] [5]

## Reviews

Uber Eats shows a 4.1 rating from 56 ratings and includes recent positive comments about portions, price, presentation, and taste. One Bite shows a 7.3 rating based on four reviews, while the legacy Yelp search result reports a larger review and photo presence under Best Pizza. These sources indicate existing customer evidence but are not interchangeable ratings. The Phase 2 site may link to review profiles and reflect recurring themes, but must not fabricate testimonials or selectively hide criticism. [4] [9] [10]

## SEO

The business can be found for branded and local ordering queries, but search results are dominated by third-party ordering pages and a legacy Best Pizza domain. The repeated address and phone help local discovery, while the name and phone conflicts weaken NAP consistency. The highest-value SEO improvement is a crawlable, client-owned site with a single canonical business identity, menu summary, location page, structured data, and clear links to the existing ordering destinations.

## Existing Website Status

No official standalone Express-owned website was verified. `orderbestpizza.com` is a Slice-powered direct ordering page, not an independently controlled marketing site. `bestpizzanewingtonct.com` is a public legacy website under the Best Pizza name and appears to be an older brand property. [1] [3]

## Third-Party Platform Dependence

The full customer path currently depends heavily on Slice and delivery marketplaces for menu browsing, item pricing, checkout, reviews, and portions of the business information. This is not a reason to remove those platforms. The Forge opportunity is to add a branded home that routes customers to the platforms that already work while also making phone calls, directions, hours, and the menu easier to understand.

## Conflicting Information

The primary conflicts are the current name, phone number, weekly hours, platform-specific pricing, and whether ice cream/scoops are an active core offering or only a marketplace category. The legacy Best Pizza site also contains testimonials and imagery whose current relevance is unknown. These are client-confirmation items, not design assumptions.

## Confidence / Source Notes

High-confidence facts are the repeated address and the phone number shown consistently by current ordering platforms. Medium-confidence facts include the current menu structure and ratings because platforms vary. Low-confidence facts include the legal/current brand transition, definitive hours, current photography rights, and the status of the “Scoops” offering.
