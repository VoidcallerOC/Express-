# Site Strategy

## Primary objective

Create an owned digital home for Express Pizza & Scoops. The future site should support **search → Express website → menu → order → call/directions** while allowing the current third-party ordering infrastructure to remain the checkout system.

## Brand direction

Use a confident neighborhood voice that treats pizza and scoops as a single local identity. Do not inherit “Best Pizza” as the primary name unless the client confirms a dual-brand or transition strategy. Avoid generic red-and-checkerboard assumptions. Final colors, typography, and logo usage require client approval.

## UX direction

The first screen should answer open/closed status, location, ordering, core offering, and phone access. The site should be designed for one-handed mobile use, short sessions, and repeat visits. Make the preferred ordering path explicit, but preserve call and directions as first-class actions.

## Information architecture

Recommended primary navigation: Home, Menu, Order Online, About, Contact/Location. Add Catering only if the business confirms active catering and can supply accurate packages or inquiry handling.

## Homepage strategy

Lead with the Express Pizza & Scoops identity, a local context line, and two or three actions: Order Online, View Menu, Call. Follow with verified popular categories, a concise “pickup and delivery” explanation, a location/hours block, and a final order CTA.

## Menu strategy

Do not recreate every marketplace modifier or pretend the site is the POS. Present a readable category overview and selected item highlights with a “See full menu / Order online” handoff. If a client-owned menu PDF or structured menu feed is supplied, link or render it with a dated “prices subject to change” note.

## Ordering strategy

Use external links to the client-confirmed preferred Slice, direct, DoorDash, Uber Eats, or Grubhub destinations. Label each option by use case, such as “Pickup / delivery ordering,” and do not build a fake cart or checkout.

## Mobile strategy

Use a sticky bottom action bar with Order, Call, and Directions. Keep key information above the fold. Avoid auto-playing video, dense carousels, tiny menu typography, and hidden hours. Ensure the site remains useful when an external ordering platform is slow or unavailable.

## Local SEO strategy

Use one canonical name, address, and phone. Add Restaurant/LocalBusiness structured data only after confirmation. Create useful title and description metadata for Newington pizza, pickup/delivery, wings, grinders, and ice cream only when each offering is verified. Add an XML sitemap, canonical URLs, accessible images, and a location section with map/directions.

## Content strategy

Write concise, specific copy about the actual business. Do not make unsupported claims about awards, ingredients, years in business, delivery radius, or customer testimonials. Use the real menu as the source of truth and record its revision date.

## Photography strategy

Prioritize current client-approved photos of signature pizza, wings or grinders, scoops/ice cream, storefront, and interior. Use public legacy imagery only after rights and currentness are confirmed. A small set of strong images is preferable to a large gallery of unknown provenance.

## Conversion strategy

Every page should lead to an appropriate next action. The highest-priority conversions are online order, phone call, directions, and menu viewing. Secondary conversion is catering inquiry if confirmed.
